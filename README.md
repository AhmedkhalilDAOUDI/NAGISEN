# NAGISEN

A SwiftUI food diary for iOS 17 and newer. It tracks meals, calories, macros, weight, body details, and daily goals. Data is saved on the device.

**Lab** is available from the bottom menu and Goals, with **LET HIM COOK** as its page title. Add bought foods from the local catalog, recent foods, online search, a package photo, or a custom nutrition label; enter servings or grams when the food has a gram weight per serving; then choose 1–7 days. The saved plan allocates only those foods across meals, tracks how much of each item it uses, shows daily calories and macros against your goal, and flags days with too little food rather than inventing ingredients. A meal can be logged directly to the diary once, including after relaunch. Changing the grocery inventory clears the previous plan so it can be regenerated. Foods entered in the existing avoidance list are excluded by name; that filter is not an allergy safety check. The planner is a simple on-device allocation tool, so review portion sizes and combinations before using it.

Lab also includes a bundled, searchable snapshot of 2,146 products listed for Morocco in Open Food Facts with 611-prefix barcodes and complete calorie, protein, carbohydrate, and fat values. It works offline and appears alongside the existing online search, which continues to search the wider database. The snapshot is community-contributed Open Food Facts data under ODbL, not a verified list of products manufactured in Morocco. Check package labels. Refresh it with `python3 tools/refresh_moroccan_catalog.py` when updating the app. The planner uses daily calories, protein, and fat from Goals, calculates carbohydrate grams from the remaining calories at 4 kcal/g, and chooses portions from the groceries you entered. If stock cannot meet a target, the plan shows the actual amounts and a gap message.

The kitchen list groups bought foods as Protein, Carb, or Fat according to the largest macro value in grams per serving. This is a quick way to organize the inventory, not a claim that a food contains only that nutrient.

The interface uses an original Japanese-inspired design. Light mode uses the icon's warm orange, near-white, and charcoal palette; dark mode uses grey, beige, and burgundy. Tap the sun or moon on the Dashboard to switch light and dark mode. The iOS Home Screen icon keeps the same sun-and-lines layout, with a red sun, grey background, and beige lines in the system's dark icon appearance.

The Dashboard includes a 5g creatine check switch. Turn it on when taken and off if you need to undo it. The check is stored for each day and resets for the next day. It is an in-app reminder, without a scheduled phone notification. Daily Balance, Nutrition, and Water share one card showing calories, macros, and hydration progress. Today's Insights appears in Goals.

A translucent glass bar at the bottom opens five sections: Dashboard, Lab, Workouts, Feed, and AI. Swipe left or right across a page to move between sections. Lab is the main home for inventory, online food search, and 1–7-day meal plans. Tap **Today** on Dashboard to open the month calendar, see a calorie ring for each day, choose a date, and review its food and workout logs. Meal logging remains available from Dashboard and Lab plans. **You** is in the upper right of Dashboard; open **Your space → Goals** for targets, Today's Insights, and a seven-day calorie chart. Workouts has activity rings, one daily set of three distinct exercises from a catalog of 112 original challenges including boxing, a workout logger with a bodybuilding exercise picker and personal records for weight and reps, dated trophies, and a Spotify card with the current track, album cover, and previous/next controls. Workout logs, daily quests, and trophies are saved locally. Music plays in Spotify, not inside NAGISEN.

Feed is a social-community interface awaiting an account and online database service. It does not show invented posts or publish to other NAGISEN users yet. You can write drafts saved on this iPhone, delete them, or share text through the iOS share sheet. A live member feed, follows, comments, and in-app publishing require a backend project.

Chat uses the [DeepSeek Chat Completions API](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/api/create-chat-completion/) for general questions. Paste your API key into Chat settings on the iPhone; it is stored in the device Keychain, not in the app source. Chat history stays on the device, and messages are sent to DeepSeek only when you tap Send. The app does not send your diary or profile to the chat unless you enable **Use my NAGISEN data** in Chat settings.

The Chat screen has **New chat** to start without earlier replies, a **Done** button above the keyboard, and swipe-down keyboard dismissal in the conversation.

Earning a new trophy opens a Japanese-print trophy reveal with falling sakura petals, a soft two-note pluck and a light haptic tap. Earned trophies can be tapped to replay the reveal. The burst is provided by [ConfettiSwiftUI](https://github.com/simibac/ConfettiSwiftUI) 3.0.0 (MIT); the trophy seal, petals, sound, and award presentation are original NAGISEN work. Reduce Motion disables the animated burst.

Apple Health can be connected from the step counter beneath the light/dark button or the Workouts tab. After on-device permission, NAGISEN reads today's steps, active calories, exercise minutes, distance, flights climbed, stand hours, latest heart rate and body weight, and recent workouts. **You → Accessories** shows recent heart-rate samples and their Health sources, along with audio accessories currently connected to the iPhone. On iOS 26 and later, an in-app HealthKit workout can collect live heart rate from AirPods Pro 3 or another compatible sensor when available and permitted. Apple Health may combine AirPods and Watch readings, so the sample may not always identify AirPods. Accessories can import workouts and weight from Health into NAGISEN, with source IDs and duplicate checks; optional automatic import runs when Health data refreshes. NAGISEN never reads arbitrary Bluetooth devices directly. Health data is displayed locally and is not sent to Spotify or AI Chat. Apple Health permissions can be changed in the Health app.

To display Spotify album art, install and sign in to Spotify on the iPhone, then tap **Connect Spotify** once in NAGISEN and authorize it. On later launches and returns to the app, NAGISEN reconnects automatically and refreshes the current track without another tap. NAGISEN uses Spotify's iOS App Remote SDK and stores its access token in the device Keychain. Spotify availability and playback state determine whether art can be shown. The public Client ID and redirect URI are already registered for this build; no Client Secret is included.

From a meal, tap **Add food**. Browse starter foods, previously logged foods, saved recipes, and previous meals; search online; scan or enter a barcode; or create a food manually. Select a food, adjust its serving or grams and meal, then tap **Add**. The plus button beside a local or saved food quickly adds one serving without leaving search; online community foods open the detail screen for label review. Log also offers direct calorie/macro entry and copying yesterday's foods. A recipe can combine foods into a reusable per-portion entry. Voice search uses on-device iOS speech recognition when available. Photo search uses on-device image classification to suggest a search term; review the food and serving before logging.

Online search and barcode lookup use [Open Food Facts](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/) (ODbL). Search runs when submitted. Product data is supplied by contributors and may be incomplete or inaccurate, so check the package label. Search terms and barcodes are sent to Open Food Facts when you use those features. Photos stay on the iPhone; Lab reads package text or a barcode on-device, then sends the recognized barcode or suggested search term to Open Food Facts. Review the returned product and nutrition before adding it. No NAGISEN account is required. The camera is used only to scan barcodes.

Open `Daywell.xcodeproj` in Xcode and run the **Daywell** scheme (the project keeps its original internal name) on an iOS 17 or newer simulator. Online lookup needs an internet connection; manual entry and saved diary data work offline.

To run on a physical iPhone, select your Apple development team under **Signing & Capabilities**.

Starter food values are approximate and stated per serving. The app does not provide medical advice. NAGISEN migrates existing history from `UserDefaults` to a local SQLite database in Application Support. The prior data remains available for migration recovery. The app makes rotating local JSON backups when it moves to the background. **You → Export my data** creates a shareable JSON backup of food, goals, weight, water, workouts, strength records, recipes, and preferences. **Restore a backup** or **Recover recent history** replaces current history after confirmation. Export does not include the DeepSeek API key, chat history, Spotify token, or raw Apple Health records. iPhone backup may include app data if enabled, but live iCloud sync is not available under the current personal development team, which cannot provision the iCloud capability.

NAGISEN's Today’s Insights card looks at logged food, protein goals, training frequency, steps, and the creatine switch to offer specific next steps. It treats missing food entries as missing data, not as proof that nothing was eaten. Side quests mix cardio, strength, skill, boxing and hybrid work, avoid recently shown names, and favor shorter challenges after a long workout day. A daily pack can be shuffled to 1–5 exercises at light, balanced, or hard difficulty, then marked done together or left for the day. A different dated trophy is earned for each completed daily pack, and finishing a shuffled pack earns the Domino Dealer trophy. Chat personalization is off by default; enabling it in Chat settings sends a short summary of goals, today’s logs, recent workouts, and permitted step count to DeepSeek with each message.

Dashboard's Done Logging switch marks a calendar day complete and shows the consecutive completed-day streak. Today's active streak remains visible until you finish logging, and completed days are included in local backups. Bonus calories show the net unused calories from earlier completed days with food entries in the same Monday–Sunday week. Completed over-goal days reduce that number; days without completed food logs add nothing. The number resets on Monday and is displayed separately. It does not change the daily balance, calorie ring, calendar, diary goal, or coaching suggestions, which use the base daily target. Water can be logged from Dashboard or Log in 250 mL and 500 mL steps, or with a custom amount. The daily target is editable, individual entries can be deleted, and optional water check-ins can be enabled from the Water details screen. The experimental Home Screen widgets are not included in the current iPhone build while their design is being reconsidered.

Your space opens a seven-day food plan from Goals or Food plan. The plan uses the latest logged weight (or Apple Health weight), age, height, a selected calorie equation, activity level, and weight goal. Choose balanced, Mediterranean, high-protein, lower-carb, keto, or a 10 a.m.–8 p.m. intermittent-fasting eating window. Each day shows meal ideas with gram portions and estimated calories and macros; meals can be added to the diary, and the week's ingredients can be shared as a shopping list. Foods to avoid can be entered as comma-separated ingredients; if no complete day fits, the plan stops instead of silently ignoring them. This is a simple name filter, not an allergy safety system. Targets can be copied into Daily Goals, which supports weekday overrides. Weekly check-in uses logged intake and a median weight trend from the last 14 days to estimate energy needs; it requires at least five days with multiple food entries in each week and four weigh-ins spanning a week, suggests at most a 100 kcal adjustment, and never changes a target automatically. The estimate is rough and partial food days can distort it. Values are food estimates, not a clinical diet prescription. The initial energy estimate uses the [Mifflin–St Jeor equation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/); [NIDDK's Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner) provides a more detailed alternative for adults. Fasting may need medical guidance with diabetes medication, pregnancy, or a history of disordered eating.

Water reminders scheduled under the former app name are replaced with NAGISEN reminders when the app next opens. Existing diary, Health, and Spotify data stays in place because the bundle ID and local storage keys are preserved internally.

When a day's logged calories exceed its goal, Dashboard shows the exact excess in the center of the calorie ring. A second arc represents the excess relative to the goal, with gold for less than 10%, warm burgundy for 10–25%, and red above 25%. Log and calendar views use the same over-goal state.

Open Food Facts and manually created foods can include fiber and sodium; the dashboard shows these only when supplied and notes that totals may be incomplete. Community foods with missing macros are flagged and can be corrected from the package label before logging. NAGISEN reads Health data and writes a workout only when the user starts and ends an in-app live workout with Health permission.
